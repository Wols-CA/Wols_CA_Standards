## 1. Purpose & change process

- One row per term. Columns: **Term** (with a stable anchor) · **Definition** (one to three sentences, may summarise but never contradict the defining document) · **Defining document** (repository path and section) · **Synonyms / NL equivalent** (for Dutch customer, legal and parliamentary conversations; English stays the master wording) · **Status**.
- **Status** values: `Draft` (proposed, awaiting Architect + SME approval) → `Approved` (merged, normative) → `Deprecated` (kept for history with a pointer to the successor term).
- Rows are ordered **strictly A–Z, case-insensitive** (digits before letters). No duplicate terms.
- Linking: other documents link to a term as `Glossary.md#<anchor>`; the anchor is the lower-case term with hyphens (see the `id` in each row).
- Rule for all Wols CA repositories and AI agents: **no artefact may introduce a term that is not in this Glossary**. Propose the term first.
- Editorial roles: the Documentalist authors changes as Pull Requests; the Architect and the Subject Matter Expert approve; a merge triggers the docs-site rebuild. Nobody edits the docs-site directly.

## 2. How to propose a change

1. On the docs-site, use **Suggest a change** on any page, or open a GitHub Issue directly with the template `.github/ISSUE_TEMPLATE/glossary_change_request.yml` in this repository (label `glossary-change-request`).
2. State the **term**, the **current wording**, your **proposed wording**, the **rationale** and whether you are a customer, partner or internal contributor. Do not include personal data.
3. The Documentalist turns the request into a Pull Request (status `Draft`); the Architect and SME review against the defining document; on merge the site is rebuilt and the Issue is closed with a link to the change.
4. Requests that would change the *meaning* of a standard are redirected to the owning standard first; the Glossary follows the standard, never the other way round.
