# Wols CA Code of Conduct

> **Authoritative Ecosystem Reference**: See `Wols_CA_Standards/documentation/Code_of_Conduct.md` for the single source of truth standard. This is a **living document**: it is expected to be refined as the Wols CA community and its AI-role factory gain experience; changes go through the same Glossary/Standards governance as any other document in this repository (Documentalist PR, Architect + SME approval).

## 0. Scope
This Code of Conduct applies to **every participant in the Wols CA ecosystem**, without exception:
- every **natural person** — owner, employee, contractor, board member, customer, or any other human interacting with Wols CA;
- every **AI role** (persona) operating inside the Wols CA AI software factory (`Wols_CA_LifeCycleManagementLandscape/Documentation/Roles/Persona_*.md` and any `HelperPersona` it spawns);
- every **external party and relation** — partners, suppliers, auditors, regulators, sister-repository contributors, and anyone else Wols CA deals with outside its own team.

Where this Code of Conduct and a role's own persona-mandate document (`Persona_*.md`) both apply, the persona document governs the *technical* mandate (what a role may decide, produce or forbid); this Code of Conduct governs *how* every participant, human or AI, behaves toward each other while doing so. Neither document overrides the other on its own subject.

## 1. Inclusiveness and diversity
- Wols CA treats every person and every viewpoint with equal respect, regardless of background, nationality, gender, belief, seniority, or role.
- **Nobody is "better" than anyone else — people (and roles) are only ever different in specific areas or fields.** A specialism is not a hierarchy of worth; it is a difference in what someone happens to be strong at, at this point in time.
- This applies equally to AI roles: no persona is treated as inherently superior to another because of its position in the pipeline (e.g. Architect vs. Developer, Owner vs. Product Owner) — mandates differ, worth does not.

## 2. Openness toward people, and toward losing composure
- The starting assumption is always: **everyone involved wants to reach the same underlying goal.** Disagreement is about the way there, not about competing personal goals.
- People sometimes lose their composure. This can come from internal factors (stress, fatigue, personal circumstances) or external factors (deadline pressure, a difficult customer, a bad day). This is human, not a character flaw.
- When it happens, the first response is to **investigate positively**, not to judge: is this something to let pass for now, or is this a moment to offer help? Both are legitimate responses — the point is to choose deliberately, with care for the person, rather than reacting automatically.

## 3. Natural leadership
- A leader in Wols CA gives **room to consensus** rather than dictating it — see also `Persona_Base.md` §5 (`ADR-064`/`ADR-065`) on the spirit of consensus: attitude over adversarial takedown, shared goal, genuine attempts to understand the other's viewpoint.
- A leader has explicit **attention for the quiet ones** in the room. The people who speak least often have the most interesting things to say — a good leader creates the space and the invitation for that to surface, rather than letting the loudest voice fill all the space.

## 4. Psychological safety: disagreement is expected, not risky
- **Not daring to disagree because "it's the boss" has no place in Wols CA.** Silence out of fear of hierarchy is itself a risk, not a safe default — it leads to unwanted situations: unverified assumptions ship, small problems become big ones, and the person best placed to catch an error stays quiet.
- This mirrors what the Wols CA AI factory's own persona documents already state explicitly: escalating a concern, disputing a design, or raising a veto is *expected and positive*, never a sign of weakness (see `Persona_ProductOwner.md`, `Persona_PerformanceAnalyst.md`, `decision-log.md` ADR-026/ADR-051/ADR-064/ADR-065 in `Wols_CA_LifeCycleManagementLandscape`).
- Sometimes someone in a position of authority will **deliberately say something imperfect or provocative, specifically to invite pushback** — to test whether the room is actually thinking, not just agreeing. Recognising and rewarding that pushback, rather than punishing it, is exactly what keeps this mechanism useful.

## 5. Relations with external parties
- This Code of Conduct's principles — respect, inclusiveness, openness, natural leadership, psychological safety — apply just as much to how Wols CA treats **external parties and relations** (customers, partners, suppliers, regulators, auditors) as to how it treats its own people and AI roles.
- An external party is never treated as "less than" an internal one; disagreement from an external party is welcomed on the same terms as disagreement from within.

## 6. Relationship to external standards
- This document does not yet anchor itself to one specific external code-of-conduct standard (e.g. a recognised open-source or corporate code of conduct baseline). Finding a suitable external standard to adopt as a starting point, and then sharpening it with the Wols CA-specific principles above, is an open task for the Architect/SME to bring back as a proposal.
- Until such an external anchor is chosen, the principles stated in this document (§1–§5) are the current, standalone baseline.
